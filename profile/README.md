## Overview
---
<p>
  <b>BioCompute Research Lab</b> is a deep-tech startup exploring a fundamental question:<br>
  <i><strong>What if biology itself could become the next computing platform?</strong></i>
</p>

<p>
Instead of using biology merely as something to study with computers, we explore how <b>biological systems can be engineered and used as computational technologies</b>.

From <i><b>DNA as an information-storage medium to living neural cells as computational substrates</b></i>, BioCompute investigates new ways of storing, processing, sensing, and transforming information using the mechanisms that biology has evolved over billions of years.
</p>

### 🧬 DNA as Storage
<p>
Digital information is ultimately represented as physical states.<br><br>

DNA offers a radically different medium for representing information: the four nucleotide bases <i><b>A, T, G, and C</b></i> can encode digital data at extraordinary molecular density.<br><br>
BioCompute explores:
</p>
<br>
<ul>
<li>DNA-based information encoding</li>
<li>Error-correcting codes for molecular storage</li>
<li>DNA synthesis and sequencing workflows</li>
<li>Retrieval and reconstruction of stored information</li>
<li>Computational architectures around DNA data storage</li>
<li>AI-assisted optimization of molecular storage systems</li>
</ul>
<br>
<p>
  <strong>The Idea:</strong> transform DNA from biological information into a programmable <i><b>information-storage medium</b></i></p>.
</p>
<br>

### 🧠 Neurons / Brain Cells as Computers
<p>
The brain demonstrates that computation does not require conventional transistors.<br>
Neurons communicate through electrical and chemical signals, learn from interactions, operate in parallel, and form adaptive networks.<br>
BioCompute explores the possibility of treating biological neural systems as computational substrates.<br>
Our research interests include:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>Neuronal computation</li>
  <li>Biological neural networks</li>
  <li>Brain-inspired computing</li>
  <li>Organoid and cellular computing</li>
  <li>Learning in living neural systems</li>
  <li>Hybrid biological--electronic interfaces</li>
  <li>Biological intelligence and emergent computation</li>
</ul><br>
<p>
  <strong>The idea</strong>: instead of only simulating neurons on a computer, investigate what happens when <b>the biological system performs the computation itself</b>.<br>
</p>
### 🦠 Cells & Microorganisms

Cells are naturally capable of sensing their environment, processing molecular signals, making decisions, and producing responses.

BioCompute explores these capabilities as a foundation for **programmable biological systems**.

<ul>
  <li>Engineering cells to respond to specific signals</li>
  <li>Cellular information processing</li>
  <li>Biological logic and decision-making</li>
  <li>Programmable microbial systems</li>
  <li>Distributed computation across populations of cells</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Core idea:</strong> Turn cellular behavior into <strong>programmable biological computation</strong>.</p><br>

### 🧪 Molecular Systems

Before computation reaches the level of cells or neural networks, information can be processed directly through **molecular interactions**.

We explore:

<ul>
  <li>Molecular logic and computation</li>
  <li>Biochemical reaction networks</li>
  <li>Molecular information processing</li>
  <li>DNA and RNA-based computation</li>
  <li>Reaction-driven decision systems</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Core idea:</strong> Use <strong>molecular interactions themselves</strong> to represent, transform, and process information.</p><br>

### 🌱 Living Systems

Biological organisms continuously sense, process information, adapt, and respond to changing environments.

BioCompute investigates whether these natural capabilities can inspire or enable **new forms of distributed and adaptive computing**.

<ul>
  <li>Biological sensing</li>
  <li>Adaptive biological systems</li>
  <li>Distributed information processing</li>
  <li>Environmental response systems</li>
  <li>Emergent computation in biological networks</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Core idea:</strong> Explore computation that is not only programmable, but also <strong>adaptive and self-organizing</strong>.</p><br>

### 🤖 AI + Biology

AI becomes an important layer across the entire BioCompute stack.

Rather than treating AI and biology as separate fields, we explore how they can work together to **design, understand, optimize, and control biological computing systems**.

We investigate:

<ul>
  <li>AI models for biological system design</li>
  <li>Machine learning for molecular and cellular systems</li>
  <li>Optimization of DNA storage architectures</li>
  <li>Modeling biological neural computation</li>
  <li>AI-assisted discovery of biological computational mechanisms</li>
  <li>Hybrid AI–biological computing architectures</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Core idea:</strong> Use AI not only to <em>analyze biology</em>, but to help <strong>design the next generation of biological computers</strong>.</p><br>

## 📈 Our research follows a simple progression:
---
<ul>
  <li><strong>Store</strong> — Can biology store digital information?</li>
  <li><strong>Compute</strong> — Can biological systems perform useful computation?</li>
  <li><strong>Sense</strong> — Can biological systems detect and interpret their environment?</li>
  <li><strong>Learn</strong> — Can biological systems adapt through experience?</li>
  <li><strong>Integrate</strong> — Can biological and electronic systems work together?</li>
  <li><strong>Scale</strong> — Can these systems become practical computing technologies?</li>
</ul>

<p align="center">
<strong>Storage → Computation → Sensing → Learning → Adaptation → Intelligence</strong>
</p><br>

## 🚀 Our Vision
---
<p>
Today's computers are built around a relatively small set of physical principles.

Biology offers an entirely different computational toolbox.

- It stores information in molecules.
- It processes signals through networks of cells.
- It learns and adapts.
- It performs massively parallel operations.
- It operates at molecular scales.
</p>
<strong>BioCompute aims to understand, engineer, and ultimately build technology around these capabilities.</strong>

<p align="center">
  <i><b>We don't just compute biology.<br>We explore computing with biology.</b></i>
</p>
<br>

## ❓The Bigger Question
---
<p>
Computing has historically evolved by finding better physical substrates for information processing.<br>
<b>Vacuum tubes → Transistors → Integrated circuits → GPUs → Specialized accelerators</b><br>
BioCompute explores another possibility:<br>

<strong>What comes after conventional computing substrates?</strong><br>

Perhaps some future computers will store information in <b>molecules</b>, perform computation using **living neural networks**, sense their environment through **cells**, and combine biological and artificial intelligence into a single system.

We are building toward that possibility.
</p>
