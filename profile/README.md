About BioCompute

BioCompute - Research Lab is a deep-tech startup exploring a fundamental question:

What if biology itself could become the next computing platform?

Instead of using biology merely as something to study with computers, we explore how biological systems can be engineered and used as computational technologies.

From DNA as an information-storage medium to living neural cells
as computational substrates, BioCompute investigates new ways of
storing, processing, sensing, and transforming information using
mechanisms that biology has evolved over billions of years.

🧬 DNA as Storage

Digital information is ultimately represented as physical states.

DNA offers a radically different medium for representing information:
the four nucleotide bases A, T, G, and C can encode digital data at
extraordinary molecular density.

BioCompute explores:

<ul>

<li>

DNA-based information encoding

</li>

<li>

Error-correcting codes for molecular storage

</li>

<li>

DNA synthesis and sequencing workflows

</li>

<li>

Retrieval and reconstruction of stored information

</li>

<li>

Computational architectures around DNA data storage

</li>

<li>

AI-assisted optimization of molecular storage systems

</li>

</ul>

<p>

<strong>{=html}The idea:</strong>{=html} transform DNA from
biological information into a programmable
<strong>{=html}information-storage medium</strong>{=html}.

</p>

🧠 Cells as Computers

The brain demonstrates that computation does not require conventional
transistors.

Neurons communicate through electrical and chemical signals, learn from
interactions, operate in parallel, and form adaptive networks.

BioCompute explores the possibility of treating biological neural
systems as computational substrates.

Our research interests include:

<ul>

<li>

Neuronal computation

</li>

<li>

Biological neural networks

</li>

<li>

Brain-inspired computing

<li>

Organoid and cellular computing

</li>

<li>

Learning in living neural systems

</li>

<li>

Hybrid biological--electronic interfaces

</li>

<li>

Biological intelligence and emergent computation

</li>

</ul>

<strong>{=html}The idea:</strong>{=html} instead of only simulating

💡 The Bigger Question

Computing has historically evolved by finding better physical substrates
for information processing.

::: {align="center"}
Vacuum Tubes → Transistors → Integrated Circuits → GPUs → Specialized
Accelerators
:::

BioCompute explores another possibility:

::: {align="center"}

What comes after conventional computing substrates?

:::

Perhaps some future computers will store information in molecules,
perform computation using living neural networks, sense their
environment through cells, and combine biological and artificial
intelligence into a single system.

We are building toward that possibility.
